Model 1 is a multilayer perceptron (MLP) on the 561 engineered features that the dataset authors computed from every window [@anguita2013]. The features summarise the body acceleration, the gravity component of the acceleration and the angular velocity, together with their derivatives and magnitudes, in the time and frequency domains: for example means, standard deviations, energies, correlations, frequency-band energies and angles between the gravity vector and the device axes. We standardise them with statistics fitted on the 15 training subjects only.

Each hidden layer applies a linear map, batch normalisation, a ReLU and dropout, and a final linear layer produces the six class logits. The tuning grid ({{table:tuning}}) varied the dropout rate and the width of the second hidden layer. Configuration {{tuning:mlp:config_id}} was selected, with hidden widths {{tuning:mlp:hidden}} and dropout {{tuning:mlp:dropout}}, giving {{tuning:mlp:params}} trainable parameters and a validation macro-F1 of {{tuning:mlp:val_macro_f1}} on seed 42. Training follows the shared protocol of Section V: Adam with a learning rate of 0.001, batch size 64, cross-entropy loss, at most 50 epochs with early stopping on validation macro-F1 (patience 10, best weights restored) and gradient clipping at norm 1.0.

The MLP is the feed-forward baseline of the comparison. It has no notion of order inside the window: everything it knows about the temporal structure of the signal was put into the features by hand. It therefore measures how far expert feature engineering goes without any sequence modelling, and it is the reference against which the three raw-signal networks show whether features learned from the signal can replace hand-crafted ones. On the same features, a support vector machine reached 96% test accuracy in the dataset paper [@anguita2013].
