{{table:per_class}} and {{fig:per_class_f1}} give the test F1 per class. Every model recognises laying almost perfectly and the three walking classes well, with the 1D-CNN strongest on all three. The errors concentrate in sitting and standing: for sitting, the raw-signal models range from {{analysis:per_class_f1.gru.SITTING}} (GRU) to {{analysis:per_class_f1.cnn1d.SITTING}} (1D-CNN), against {{analysis:per_class_f1.mlp.SITTING}} for the MLP.

Counting errors per seed shows the same pattern. Sitting predicted as standing, or the reverse, makes up between {{analysis:error_structure.mlp.sitting_standing_share|%}} (MLP) and {{analysis:error_structure.cnn1d.sitting_standing_share|%}} (1D-CNN) of each model's test errors. The MLP makes {{analysis:error_structure.mlp.sitting_standing|.1f}} such errors, against {{analysis:error_structure.cnn1d.sitting_standing|.1f}} to {{analysis:error_structure.gru.sitting_standing|.1f}} for the raw-signal models, but it confuses the walking classes more often ({{analysis:error_structure.mlp.walking|.1f}} errors, against {{analysis:error_structure.cnn1d.walking|.1f}} for the 1D-CNN); the Transformer confuses them most ({{analysis:error_structure.transformer.walking|.1f}}).

The models' errors overlap far more than chance. Over all pairs of models, the Jaccard overlap of the misclassified test windows averages {{analysis:overlap.jaccard_mean}}, against {{analysis:overlap.jaccard_chance_mean}} if each model erred independently at its own rate ({{fig:error_overlap}}). The convolutional and recurrent networks share the most errors, led by the 1D-CNN with the GRU ({{analysis:overlap.pairs.0.jaccard}}) and with the BiLSTM ({{analysis:overlap.pairs.1.jaccard}}), while the MLP overlaps least with every other model ({{analysis:overlap.jaccard.mlp.transformer}} to {{analysis:overlap.jaccard.mlp.bilstm}}). Per seed, {{analysis:overlap.all_wrong|.1f}} windows are misclassified by all five models, and {{analysis:overlap.all_wrong_sitting_standing_share|%}} of them are sitting or standing. At the other end, {{analysis:overlap.exactly_one|.1f}} windows are misclassified by exactly one model, mostly by the Transformer ({{analysis:overlap.exactly_one_by_model.transformer|.1f}}) and the MLP ({{analysis:overlap.exactly_one_by_model.mlp|.1f}}), whereas almost every error of the 1D-CNN is shared with another model ({{analysis:overlap.exactly_one_by_model.cnn1d|.1f}} of its own).

Only {{analysis:confusion_cells.n_shared}} confusion cells hold at least {{analysis:confusion_cells.threshold|%}} of a class's test windows for every model: sitting predicted as standing and standing predicted as sitting. The {{analysis:confusion_cells.n_model_specific}} cells that reach this level for only one or two models all involve a walking class. The two that cross from a static posture to a dynamic activity appear only for the recurrent models: laying predicted as walking upstairs (BiLSTM {{analysis:confusion_cells.model_specific.2.share.bilstm|%}}, GRU {{analysis:confusion_cells.model_specific.2.share.gru|%}}) and sitting predicted as walking upstairs (GRU {{analysis:confusion_cells.model_specific.3.share.gru|%}}).

Objective 3 therefore has a two-part answer. A core of errors is shared and intrinsic to the sensor representation: sitting and standing are both static postures with the phone at the waist, every family confuses them, and almost all windows that defeat every model belong to these two classes. Beyond that core the errors depend on the architecture. The engineered features reduce the sitting and standing confusion well below that of any raw-signal model but confuse the walking classes more often than the convolutional and recurrent networks, these two families share the largest part of their errors, and only the recurrent networks mistake static postures for walking upstairs in at least {{analysis:confusion_cells.threshold|%}} of a class's windows. {{fig:confusion_best}} shows the summed test confusion matrix of the MLP, the model with the best mean test macro-F1.
