We use the UCI HAR dataset [@ucihar2013; @anguita2013]: 30 volunteers performed six activities (walking, walking upstairs, walking downstairs, sitting, standing and laying) with a smartphone at the waist. Its accelerometer and gyroscope signals, sampled at 50 Hz, are cut into 10,299 windows of 128 samples (2.56 s) with 50% overlap.

Two representations are built from the same windows. The raw representation keeps 9 channels (body acceleration, angular velocity and total acceleration, each on three axes) as a 128 by 9 sequence, standardised per channel. The feature representation is the 561 time- and frequency-domain features supplied with the dataset, standardised per feature. Labels 1 to 6 become class indices 0 to 5. Data augmentation is implemented but switched off in every reported run.

We keep the official subject-independent split: 21 training subjects with 7,352 windows, and 9 test subjects ({{split:test_subjects}}) with 2,947 windows. Six training subjects ({{split:val_subjects}}), drawn once with seed 42, form the validation set with 2,076 windows, which leaves 15 subjects and 5,276 windows for training. Because neighbouring windows overlap, a window-level split would put near-identical windows on both sides; splitting by subject prevents this leakage. Both scalers are fitted on the 15 training subjects only. No activity is rare in any split ({{fig:class_distribution}}).

Every model is trained with one protocol: Adam with a learning rate of 0.001, batch size 64, cross-entropy loss, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, and at most 50 epochs with early stopping on validation macro-F1 (patience 10, best weights restored). Tuning changes only model hyperparameters, with the same budget for every model: a grid of four configurations over two hyperparameters, starting from the default ({{table:tuning}}). Each configuration is trained once with seed 42 and scored on validation only. The best validation macro-F1 wins; configurations within 0.001 of it count as tied, and a tie goes to fewer parameters, then to the default. The selected configuration of each model is trained again with seeds 42, 43 and 44, which change the initialisation, dropout masks and batch order, and every result is the mean and sample standard deviation of the three runs.

We report accuracy, macro-F1 (the unweighted mean of the per-class F1 scores), per-class F1, confusion matrices, trainable parameters, training time and inference latency. The test subjects are used only for reporting: every choice of configuration, epoch and checkpoint is made on validation, and each final checkpoint is evaluated once on the test split.

All runs used one laptop: an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU for training and an AMD Ryzen 7 7840HS CPU. Latency is the mean of 200 single-thread CPU forward passes at batch size 1 after 20 warm-up passes, measured for all five final models in one process.
