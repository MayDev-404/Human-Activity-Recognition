Human activity recognition from smartphone inertial signals can be addressed with feed-forward, convolutional, recurrent or attention-based networks, but published results are hard to compare because protocols differ and random splits let the same subjects appear in training and test data. We compare four families under one subject-independent protocol on the UCI HAR dataset: a multilayer perceptron (MLP) on the 561 engineered features, and a 1D-CNN, a bidirectional LSTM with a GRU ablation and a Transformer encoder on the raw 9-channel windows. Every model is tuned on validation subjects with the same budget of four configurations and trained with three seeds; the test subjects are used only for reporting. The MLP reached the highest test macro-F1 ({{final:mlp:test_macro_f1}}), ahead of the 1D-CNN ({{final:cnn1d:test_macro_f1}}), the BiLSTM ({{final:bilstm:test_macro_f1}}), the GRU ({{final:gru:test_macro_f1}}) and the Transformer ({{final:transformer:test_macro_f1}}), and it had the lowest CPU latency, although its features must be computed first. The raw-signal networks reached similar or higher validation scores but lost more from validation to test. Errors overlapped far more than chance predicts (mean pairwise Jaccard {{analysis:overlap.jaccard_mean|.2f}}, against {{analysis:overlap.jaccard_chance_mean|.2f}} for independent errors), and {{analysis:overlap.all_wrong_sitting_standing_share|%}} of the windows that every model misclassified were sitting or standing. A core of static-posture confusion is therefore shared by all architectures, and engineered features remain a strong and cheap baseline under subject-independent evaluation.
