Hand-crafted features set the first reference point on UCI HAR. The dataset paper trained a one-vs-all support vector machine with a Gaussian kernel on 561 time- and frequency-domain features and reached 96% accuracy on the 2,947 test windows, with sitting the hardest class (88% recall) [@anguita2013]. The same group later added postural transitions to the recordings and handled them with a probabilistic SVM and temporal filtering, with a system error of 3.22% when transitions were learned and 3.64% when they were treated as unknown [@reyesortiz2016]. Feature pipelines are therefore strong, but their features are designed by hand, and fixed windows cope poorly with transitions.

Convolutional networks learn features from the raw signals instead. Temporal convolution and pooling over multichannel recordings outperformed the best baseline on OPPORTUNITY by about 5% [@yang2015]. On UCI HAR, a deep 1D convnet on raw accelerometer and gyroscope signals reached 94.79% test accuracy, and 95.75% when temporal FFT features were added [@ronao2016]. A two-layer CNN on two-dimensional DFT "activity images" reached 95.18%, or 97.59% with an SVM on top, which shows how strongly the input representation affects a CNN [@jiang2015].

Recurrent networks model the order and duration of movements across the window. Deep LSTM networks in unidirectional, bidirectional and cascaded forms were compared on five datasets, with 96.7% on UCI HAR for a four-layer unidirectional LSTM [@murad2017]. Three stacked residual bidirectional LSTM layers reached 93.6% accuracy and 93.5% F1 on UCI HAR [@zhao2018]. Hybrids combine both families: DeepConvLSTM feeds four convolutional layers into two LSTM layers and reached a weighted F1 of 0.930 for locomotion on OPPORTUNITY (without the Null class) and 0.958 on Skoda [@ordonez2016], and convolution placed after two LSTM layers gave 95.78% on UCI HAR [@xia2020].

Attention-based models drop recurrence altogether. A self-attention network with attention across sensor modalities reached a window-wise macro-F1 of 0.96 on PAMAP2 and 0.97 on Skoda, but only 0.67 on OPPORTUNITY and 0.55 on USC-HAD [@mahmud2020]. A Transformer encoder on accelerometer and gyroscope sequences from KU-HAR reached 99.2% average accuracy against 89.67% for a random forest, but its random 70:15:15 split places windows of the same person in both training and test data [@dirgova2022].

Comparisons across families find that none wins everywhere. In more than 4,000 experiments with MLPs, CNNs, LSTMs and bidirectional LSTMs, the bidirectional LSTM was best on OPPORTUNITY (mean F1 0.745) and the CNN on PAMAP2 (mean F1 0.937), so the best family depended on the type of activity [@hammerla2016]. A survey of deep learning for sensor-based recognition reached the same conclusion: CNNs led on the UCI smartphone data, recurrent networks suited short ordered activities, and hybrids often beat single models [@wang2019].

These results are hard to set against each other. Deep models on UCI HAR report between 93.6% and 97.59% [@zhao2018; @jiang2015], but they differ in input representation and architecture, and the strongest attention result relies on a split that is not subject-wise. None of the studies reviewed compares feed-forward, convolutional, recurrent and attention-based networks on UCI HAR under one subject-independent protocol, with an equal tuning budget, several seeds and an analysis of which errors the families share. This project fills that gap.
