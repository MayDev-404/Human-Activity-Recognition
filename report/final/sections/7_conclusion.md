On the first objective, the MLP on engineered features had the highest test macro-F1 ({{final:mlp:test_macro_f1}}) and the smallest spread over seeds, followed closely by the 1D-CNN ({{final:cnn1d:test_macro_f1}}), with the BiLSTM, its GRU ablation and the Transformer further behind. The 1D-CNN and the GRU had the highest validation macro-F1, so the validation subjects did not predict the ranking on new subjects. The MLP's lead comes from sitting and standing, which its gravity and angle features plausibly describe more directly, while the 1D-CNN was better on the walking classes ({{table:per_class}}).

On the second objective, the MLP was also cheapest at inference ({{final:mlp:cpu_latency_bs1_ms}} ms per window on one CPU thread), but that excludes computing its features. Among the raw-signal models the 1D-CNN combined the best accuracy with the lowest latency ({{final:cnn1d:cpu_latency_bs1_ms}} ms), so it is the practical choice when features are not computed on the device. The GRU was slower than the BiLSTM because PyTorch accelerates only the LSTM with oneDNN on CPU; with oneDNN disabled both took the same time.

On the third objective, errors overlapped far more than chance and shared one core: {{analysis:overlap.all_wrong|.1f}} windows per seed were misclassified by all five models, {{analysis:overlap.all_wrong_sitting_standing_share|%}} of them sitting or standing. This core points to a limit of the waist-worn signal rather than of any architecture. The engineered features reduce the sitting and standing confusion without removing it ({{analysis:error_structure.mlp.sitting_standing|.1f}} such errors per seed, against {{analysis:error_structure.cnn1d.sitting_standing|.1f}} for the 1D-CNN), and the confusions specific to one or two models all involve the walking classes.
